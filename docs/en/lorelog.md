# Lorelog

*A Standard Notation for Worldbuilding Logs*

Sep 25, 2026 · @Roberto Bisceglie

## 1. Introduction

Lorelog is a lightweight shorthand for recording how a fictional world gets built: the questions you ask, the facts you decide, the tensions those facts create, and how each decision spreads through the rest of the setting.

Worldbuilding has the same problem solo play has. Decisions pile up fast, and a week later you cannot remember why the harbor is walled, who decided the Guild pays its wardens per arrest, or which fact you quietly changed in Session 3. Prose notes bury the decisions. Bullet lists lose the reasons. Lorelog records the reasoning chain itself, so the world document stays lean and the log keeps the history.

### 1.1 Lorelog and Lonelog

Lorelog is a sibling of **Lonelog**, the notation for solo RPG session logging. The two share a family resemblance and one practical component: the **tag grammar** for persistent elements (`[N:Name|tags]`, `[L:Name|tags]` and so on). That shared grammar means a play log and a world log can sit in the same notebook or vault and stay searchable together.

The core symbols are different, because the activity is different. Lonelog records **uncertainty resolved through play**: an action, a roll, a consequence. Lorelog records **a world decided through questions**: a question or a need, a fact, the friction it creates, and its ripple through the setting.

### 1.2 What Lorelog Does

Lorelog helps you:

- **Record decisions with their reasons**, so you can revisit them without guesswork
- **Keep tension visible**, so the world stays full of conflict instead of settling into a tidy encyclopedia
- **Track the backlog** of open questions and story needs
- **Trace changes** when you revise or retract a fact
- **Share your process** with collaborators or readers who know the notation

The notation is designed to be:

- **Method-agnostic:** it implements the Lean Worldbuilding Method (both Story Mode and Exploration Mode), and works equally well for generative worldbuilding games, campaign prep, or free exploration
- **Layered:** usable as bare shorthand or with rich notes
- **Searchable:** tags and prefixes make every element easy to find
- **Format-agnostic:** it works in Markdown files and in paper notebooks

### 1.3 How to Use This Notation

This is a **toolbox, not a rulebook**. At its core are five symbols:

- `?` for a question you want answered
- `!` for a need a story imposes
- `=` for a fact you establish
- `~` for the friction that fact creates
- `>>` for the ripple that fact sends through the world

Everything else is optional: modifiers, tags, headers, phases, tests. Think in concentric circles:

- **Core Notation** (required): triggers, facts, frictions, ripples
- **Modifiers and Tags** (add as needed): provisional facts, retractions, methods, tests, persistent elements
- **Structure** (for organization): world header, build session header, cycles, phases

**Start small.** Log one question with the core symbols. Add layers when they serve you.

**A note on licensing:** The CC BY-SA 4.0 license covers this specification. Worlds, logs, and settings you create with Lorelog are your own work. License them however you like.

### 1.4 Quick Start

```
? A question about your world
= The answer you decide
~ What goes wrong because of it
>> What else it changes
```

#### Quick Start Example

```
? Who controls fresh water in the city?
= The Cistern Guild owns every public well
~ The Guild raises prices in dry seasons. Riots follow.
>> [F:Cistern Guild|monopoly] [Tension:Water riots|seasonal]
? Who enforces the Guild's rights during a riot?
```

That last line is a new question, generated by the ripple. This is the engine of Lorelog: every good answer opens more questions than it closes.

## 2. Digital vs Analog Formats

Lorelog works in Markdown files and in paper notebooks. The notation is identical; only the wrapping differs.

### 2.1 Digital Format (Markdown)

- **World metadata** goes in YAML front matter
- **World title** is a level 1 heading
- **Build sessions** are level 2 headings (`## Build 3`)
- **Cycles** are level 3 headings (`### Q7`)
- **Notation** goes in code blocks
- **Notes and prose** go between code blocks

**Always wrap notation in code blocks.** Two Lorelog symbols have a meaning in Markdown outside code blocks: a line of `=` under a line of text turns that text into a heading, and `>>` at the start of a line opens a nested blockquote. Inside a code block, both are safe.

### 2.2 Analog Format (Notebooks)

- Write headers as shown in Section 6
- Write notation directly, without code fences
- Brackets and prefixes make the page easy to scan

On paper, a small margin mark next to open `?` and `!` lines (a box you tick when answered) turns any page into a visual backlog.

### 2.3 Indentation

Indentation is optional and carries no structural meaning. Indenting the lines that belong to one cycle helps scanning:

```
? Who controls fresh water in the city?
    = The Cistern Guild owns every public well
    ~ Prices rise in dry seasons. Riots follow.
    >> [F:Cistern Guild|monopoly]
```

## 3. Core Notation

Five symbols follow the natural loop of building a world: something demands an answer, you decide the answer, you find where it hurts, and you see what else it touches.

```
? or !   ->   =   ->   ~   ->   >>   ->   new ? lines
trigger      fact     friction   ripple
```

### 3.1 Triggers

Detail should never appear from nowhere. Every addition to a world answers something. Lorelog has two kinds of trigger, one for each source of demand.

**Questions (`?`)** come from curiosity. You want to understand how the world works.

```
? How does the Guild police unlicensed practitioners?
? What do farmers eat in winter?
? Why did the old empire fall?
```

**Needs (`!`)** come from a story. A scene, a chapter, or an adventure requires something the world does not yet contain. Name the story unit that creates the need:

```
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the city gate
! Adv2 needs a faction that profits from the war
! S14 needs a way to cross the marsh in one night
```

The difference matters. A question can be answered in many ways, and the answer should follow the world's logic. A need has a success condition: the answer must make the scene work. When a need is too broad, break it into questions:

```
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the gate
? Who guards the gates?
? What do they check?
```

You can point a trigger at the section of your world document it belongs to: `? Who guards the gates? (§II)`.

### 3.2 Facts

The `=` symbol establishes something true about the world. This is the **surface layer**: the plain answer.

```
= District wardens enforce Guild licenses, paid per arrest
= Winter staples are dried fish and barley
= The empire fell when its roads were abandoned to the forest
```

Keep one fact per line. If an answer has several parts, write several `=` lines. Separate facts can be tagged, changed, or retracted one by one.

A fact without a trigger is allowed, for example during the Seed phase or when an idea arrives on its own. Consider marking it provisional (`=?`, see Section 4) until something asks for it.

### 3.3 Friction

The `~` symbol records the **tension layer**: what goes wrong because the fact is true. Who pays for it? Who resists it? What breaks under pressure?

```
= District wardens enforce Guild licenses, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests. False accusations are common.
~ Outer districts hide their healers instead of reporting them.
```

The wave shape is deliberate: friction is where the world stops being still.

Friction is the heart of Lorelog. A world made only of facts is an encyclopedia. A world made of facts and frictions generates stories. **A cycle with no `~` line is a warning sign**: the answer may resolve too much. It is not an error, but it deserves a second look.

### 3.4 Ripples

The `>>` symbol records how the fact propagates: which other elements change, which tags update, which sections are affected.

```
>> [F:Guild|+wardens]
>> [Tension:False arrests|open]
>> §IV: common people see the Guild as a predator, not a protector
```

New questions that the fact raises go on `?` lines right after the ripple. Their position tells you they were generated by this cycle:

```
>> [F:Guild|+wardens]
? Who protects people from the wardens?
? What does an arrest cost a family?
```

### 3.5 Complete Cycles

#### Exploration cycle

```
? How does the Guild police unlicensed practitioners?
= District wardens, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests. False accusations are common.
>> [F:Guild|+wardens] [Tension:False arrests|open]
? Who protects people from the wardens?
```

#### Story cycle

```
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the gate
= Every traveler must show a Guild license or a warden's pass
~ Passes are sold on the black market. Forgeries are everywhere.
>> [L:North Gate|checkpoint] [Tension:Forged passes|open]
```

#### A cycle that resolves too much

```
? What happens to people who cannot pay for a license?
= The Guild waives the fee for the poor
(note: no friction. Who decides who is poor? Try again.)
= The Guild waives the fee for anyone a temple vouches for
~ Temples sell their vouchers. Faith becomes a fee.
```

## 4. Modifiers

The core symbols describe what the world is. Modifiers describe how firm a fact is, how you reached it, and whether it still holds. All of them are optional.

### 4.1 Provisional Facts (`=?`)

A provisional fact is a working assumption: true for now, open to change without ceremony.

```
=? The capital sits on a river delta
=? Magic is rare, maybe one person in a thousand
```

Use it for Seed-phase keywords, for ideas you like but have not tested, and for facts nothing has asked for yet. When a fact survives a test or a trigger relies on it, confirm it by writing it again as `=`:

```
=? The capital sits on a river delta
... later ...
! Ch5 needs a flood that isolates the lower city
= The capital sits on a river delta (confirmed)
```

### 4.2 Retractions (`x=`)

Worlds change. A fact you retract is not erased; it is marked, with a reason. This is what makes the log a real Evolution Log.

```
x= Magic is free to practice
(why: Ch7 needs a black market for spells)
= Magic requires a Guild license, renewed yearly
~ Unlicensed casters trade services at night markets
>> [Rule:Magic|free→licensed] [L:Night Market|new]
```

The `x=` line quotes the old fact. The note gives the reason. The new `=` line replaces it. Six months later, you can find every retraction with one search and see why the world is the way it is.

To revise a fact partially, use the tag update syntax instead: `[L:Harbor|pop 40k→60k]`.

### 4.3 Method (`via:`)

The `via:` prefix records how an answer was reached. It is optional, like roll details in Lonelog.

```
via: decide                    (you chose it)
via: tbl Settlement Size d6=4  (a random table)
via: gen NPC Faction           (a multi-step generator)
via: ref Braudel, The Mediterranean  (a real-world source or model)
via: play Lonelog S12          (it emerged during play)
via: collab Anna               (a co-author proposed it)
```

Place `via:` between the trigger and the fact:

```
? What sits at the crossroads north of the capital?
via: tbl Landmark d8=6
= A ruined toll tower, half-swallowed by ivy
~ The Guild claims the tower. The local lord claims the road.
```

Random tables work exactly as in Lonelog. You can define them inline, use filtered option sets, and write multi-line generator results:

```
tbl: Landmark (d8)
  1-2: Shrine
  3-4: Well
  5-6: Ruined toll tower
  7: Standing stones
  8: Nothing, and that is strange

gen: Faction
  Wants: d6=2 -> Control of trade
  Fears: d6=5 -> Exposure of its founder
  Method: d6=1 -> Debt
= [F:Ledger House|creditor to half the nobility]
```

### 4.4 Tests (`t:`)

The `t:` prefix records a completion test and its result. The format is `t: TestName [subject] -> Result`.

```
t: Tuesday [guild practitioner, capital] -> Pass
t: Tuesday [dockworker, outer district] -> Gap: where does the food come from?
t: Scene [Ch3 gate check, 500 words] -> Pass
t: Pitch [60 seconds] -> Fail: too many factions to explain
```

A `Gap` or `Fail` result creates a trigger. Write it on the next line:

```
t: Tuesday [dockworker, outer district] -> Gap: food source
? Where does the outer district get its food?
```

Section 7 describes the tests of the Lean Worldbuilding Method. You can define your own tests for any method.

### 4.5 Notes

Meta notes use parentheses, as in Lonelog. Two note types are especially useful in worldbuilding:

```
(why: the reason for a decision or retraction)
(note: a comment, doubt, or reminder)
(parked: an idea you do not want to lose, not yet part of the world)
```

`(parked: ...)` is the honest home for ideas that nothing has asked for yet. Parking an idea keeps it out of the world document without losing it.

## 5. Persistent Elements

Tags track the elements of your world across cycles and sessions: factions, places, people, rules, tensions, open questions. The grammar is the same as Lonelog's, so tags from play logs and world logs can be searched together.

### 5.1 Tag Grammar (shared with Lonelog)

```
[Type:Name|tag|tag]           first mention
[#Type:Name]                  reference to an earlier mention
[Type:Name|old→new]           explicit change
[Type:Name|+tag] [Type:Name|-tag]   add or remove a tag
[Type:Name|cat:value,value|cat:value]   categories
```

Multi-line tags work as in Lonelog:

```
[F:Cistern Guild
  | wants: monopoly, legitimacy
  | fears: drought ending, rival wells
  | method: pricing, wardens
]
```

### 5.2 Tag Types

| Tag | Element | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `[F:Name]` | Faction, institution, group | `[F:Cistern Guild\|monopoly]` |
| `[N:Name]` | Named person | `[N:Warden Ossa\|corrupt\|ambitious]` |
| `[L:Name]` | Location | `[L:North Gate\|checkpoint]` |
| `[Rule:Name]` | World rule (how something works) | `[Rule:Magic\|licensed\|costs memory]` |
| `[Tension:Name]` | Persistent friction | `[Tension:Water riots\|seasonal]` |
| `[Hist:Name]` | Historical event | `[Hist:Fall of the Roads\|year:-212]` |
| `[Term:Name]` | In-world term or concept | `[Term:vouch\|temple license waiver]` |
| `[Q:Name]` | Backlog question | `[Q:Warden oversight\|open]` |
| `[Need:Name]` | Backlog story need | `[Need:Ch3 gate stop\|open]` |

`[N:]` and `[L:]` mean the same as in Lonelog, so a location built in the world log keeps its identity when a character walks into it during play. `[Hist:]` avoids Lonelog's `[E:]` (Event/Clock), which tracks progress during play, not history.

Custom tag types are allowed. Declare them in the world header (Section 6) so readers know what they mean.

### 5.3 States for Tensions, Questions, and Needs

Three tag types carry a state that changes over time.

**Tensions:**

- `latent`: exists but has not erupted
- `open`: active and visible
- `seasonal` or `cyclic`: erupts on a pattern
- `resolved`: settled (rare, and worth a friction check: who lost?)

**Questions and needs:**

- `open`: waiting for an answer
- `answered`: resolved, optionally with a pointer (`[Q:Warden oversight|answered→C9]`)
- `dropped`: no longer worth answering

### 5.4 Section References

When your world lives in a structured document, such as an LWM template, point at its sections with `§`:

```
? Who guards the gates? (§II)
>> §IV: common people fear the wardens more than bandits
```

### 5.5 One Element, Two Logs

The shared grammar is what connects building and playing. A fact that emerges during play can come back into the world, with its origin recorded:

```
(Lonelog, session 12)
? Is the North Gate guarded at night?
-> No, but...
=> Only a bribed warden, asleep. [L:North Gate|unguarded at night]

(Lorelog, build 5)
? Why is the North Gate unguarded at night?
via: play Lonelog S12
= The night shift is sold to smugglers by the warden captain
~ Honest wardens refuse the shift. They are transferred to the marshes.
>> [L:North Gate|smuggling route] [F:Wardens|+split]
```

## 6. Optional Structure

Structure helps you find things later. It also costs time. Use the levels that keep you oriented and skip the rest.

### 6.1 World Header

The world header is the cover page of your log. It states what you are building, with what method, and around which central tension.

**Digital (YAML front matter):**

```yaml
title: The Cistern Cities
method: LWM Exploration Mode
author: Roberto
genre: Low fantasy, urban
pitch: A drought-stricken city where water is law and the law is for sale
central_tension: The Guild keeps the city alive and keeps it poor
world_doc: cistern-cities.md
custom_tags: [Guild:] guild ranks
start_date: 2026-09-25
last_update: 2026-09-25
```

**Analog:**

```
=== World Log: The Cistern Cities ===
[Method]           LWM Exploration Mode
[Genre]            Low fantasy, urban
[Pitch]            A drought-stricken city where water is law and the law is for sale
[Central Tension]  The Guild keeps the city alive and keeps it poor
[World Doc]        Notebook 2, pages 1-12
[Start Date]       2026-09-25
```

The `method` field can be `LWM Story Mode`, `LWM Exploration Mode`, the name of a generative game, `campaign prep`, or anything else. The `world_doc` field points to the document the log feeds, if one exists.

### 6.2 Build Session Header

A build session is one sitting of work on the world.

**Digital:**

```markdown
## Build 3
*Date: 2026-09-27 | Duration: 1h | Phase: Skeleton | Cycles: C12-C18*

**Focus:** Section II, enforcement and resistance
```

**Analog:**

```
=== Build 3 ===
[Date]      2026-09-27
[Duration]  1h
[Phase]     Skeleton
[Focus]     Section II, enforcement and resistance
```

### 6.3 Cycles

A **cycle** is the unit of work: one trigger and everything that follows from it. Cycles are numbered `C1`, `C2`, `C3` and continue across the whole world, so `C47` points to one place in the log forever.

```
C7 *Guild enforcement* (§II)
? How does the Guild police unlicensed practitioners?
= District wardens, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests. False accusations are common.
>> [F:Guild|+wardens] [Tension:False arrests|open]
? Who protects people from the wardens?
```

The title in asterisks names the topic. The `§` reference names the section of the world document it touches.

**Sub-cycles (`C7.1`, `C7.2`)** handle a burst of quick answers under one topic, for example seeding keywords:

```
C1 *Seed: Section I, Power*
C1.1 ? Who rules? =? The Cistern Guild
C1.2 ? How? =? Water pricing
C1.3 ? Who resists? =? Temple of the Rain
```

**Branches (`C7a`, `C7b`)** explore alternative answers to the same trigger before you commit. Mark the one you keep:

```
C7a *Guild enforcement, option A*
= The Guild hires mercenaries
~ Mercenaries switch sides when pay is late

C7b *Guild enforcement, option B* (kept)
= District wardens, paid per arrest
~ Wardens profit from arrests
```

Branches are the log's answer to a question worldbuilders ask constantly: *what if it were different?* The rejected branch stays in the log, visible and recoverable.

### 6.4 Modules

When a world grows past one document (LWM suggests splitting past 3,000 words), prefix cycles with a module code: `M1-C4`, `M2-C1`. Name modules in the world header.

```
modules: M1 The City, M2 The Marshes, M3 The Rain Faith
```

### 6.5 Phase Markers

For methods with phases, mark transitions with a line:

```
=== Phase 2: Skeleton ===
```

A phase gate is a good place for a test (`t:`) and a note on the decision:

```
t: Pitch [60 seconds] -> Pass
t: Generative depth [answer 1 question, 2 new appear] -> Pass
(note: gate passed, moving to Skeleton)
=== Phase 2: Skeleton ===
```

## 7. Using Lorelog with the Lean Worldbuilding Method

Lorelog was designed to implement both modes of the Lean Worldbuilding Method (LWM). The world document holds the current state of the setting. The Lorelog log holds how it got there.

### 7.1 Concept Mapping

| LWM concept | Lorelog notation |
| --- | --- |
| Story need (Story Mode) | `!` trigger, `[Need:]` tag |
| Queued question (Exploration Mode) | `?` trigger, `[Q:]` tag |
| Two-Layer Rule, surface answer | `=` fact |
| Two-Layer Rule, tension answer | `~` friction |
| New questions generated | `>>` ripple, then `?` lines |
| Evolution Log | The log itself, plus `x=` and `(why: ...)` |
| Question Log | The chain of cycles: `?` then `=` then new `?` |
| Seed keywords | `=?` provisional facts in sub-cycles |
| Scene test, Inhabitant Test, pitch | `t:` lines |
| Decision gates | Phase markers with `t:` lines |
| Persistent Frictions (§II) | `[Tension:]` tags |
| Section VI (Mechanism, Cost, Golden Rule) | `[Rule:]` tags with categories |

### 7.2 Story Mode

In Story Mode, every addition starts from `!`. The log becomes the Evolution Log the method asks for: a clear chain from story need to world detail.

```
C21 *Gate checks* (§I, §VI)
! Ch3 needs a reason for the hero to be stopped at the gate
= Every traveler must show a Guild license or a warden's pass
~ Passes are sold on the black market
>> [L:North Gate|checkpoint] [Need:Ch3 gate stop|answered]
t: Scene [Ch3 gate check, 500 words] -> Pass
```

The Story Mode anti-patterns become visible in the log:

- **Detail "for completeness"** is a `=` line with no `!` above it. Park it: `(parked: ...)`.
- **Worldbuilding before the scene needs it** is a `!` that names no story unit. Name the chapter or scene, or turn it into a `?`.
- **Answering questions the story won't ask** is a `?` in a Story Mode log. It is allowed, but it is a signal that you are exploring.

The Conflict Generator test from Phase 2 has its own line:

```
t: Hooks [§II Conflict Generator] -> 3: false arrests, forged passes, water riots
```

### 7.3 Exploration Mode

In Exploration Mode, every addition starts from `?`, pulled from the backlog. The log becomes the Question Log: `Question → Answer → New Questions`, with depth visible as a chain.

#### The backlog

The backlog is every `[Q:]` tag with state `open`. You do not need a separate list: a search for `|open]` produces it. At the start of a build session, you can copy a snapshot:

```
(backlog snapshot, Build 4)
[Q:Warden oversight|open|p1]
[Q:Arrest cost|open|p2]
[Q:Outer district food|open|p1]
[Q:Rain Temple doctrine|open|p3]
```

The optional `p1`, `p2`, `p3` tags set priority. Pull from the top.

Pruning is a state change with a reason:

```
[Q:Rain Temple doctrine|open→dropped]
(why: open for 3 months, nothing depends on it)
```

#### The Inhabitant Test

The Inhabitant Test has five questions. A multi-line `t:` block records each answer, using `=` where the document already answers and `Gap` where it does not:

```
t: Tuesday [dockworker, outer district]
  Wakes: = tenement by the dry canal
  Eats: Gap
  Friction before noon: = the water queue at the Guild well
  Depends on: = the foreman who holds his work token
  Fears: = a warden raid on the tenement
-> Gap
? Where does the outer district get its food?
[Q:Outer district food|open|p1]
```

Every `Gap` becomes a new backlog entry. When all five lines hold `=`, the result is `Pass`.

#### Generative depth

The Seed-phase gate asks whether answering one question makes two more appear. The log shows this directly: count the `?` lines after a cycle's ripple. A cycle that generates none, and has no `~` line, is the resolved kind of detail the method warns about.

```
t: Generative depth [C1-C6] -> Pass: 6 cycles, 11 new questions
```

### 7.4 Switching Modes

Many worlds start in Exploration Mode and move to Story Mode when a story appears. The log can hold both. Record the switch in the header (`last_method:`) or with a note, and the two trigger types keep the distinction clear: `?` lines are curiosity, `!` lines are story.

## 8. Beyond LWM

The loop of trigger, fact, friction, and ripple is not specific to one method. Any activity where a world is decided step by step can use Lorelog.

### 8.1 Generative Worldbuilding Games

Many games build a world through prompts, cards, or turns. The prompt is the trigger. The answer is the fact. The game's own consequences become frictions and ripples.

```
C4 *Spring, week 3*
? (prompt) A stranger arrives with news of a far-off danger. What is it?
via: game card draw
= Traders report that the upriver dam is cracking
~ The Guild denies it. Denial protects its prices.
>> [Tension:Dam collapse|latent] [Clock:Dam 1/6]
```

Progress elements from Lonelog (`[Clock:]`, `[Track:]`, `[Timer:]`) work here unchanged, for games that track pressure over turns.

### 8.2 Collaborative Worldbuilding

When several people build together, attribute any line with a parenthetical name, in the same way Lonelog attributes actions:

```
?(Anna) Is there a religion that opposes the Guild?
=(Roberto) The Temple of the Rain teaches that water is a gift, not a good
~(Anna) The Temple buys its own wells in secret
>> [F:Temple of the Rain|+secret wells]
```

The log then shows who proposed each piece, which helps when a collaborator asks why something is the way it is.

### 8.3 Campaign Prep

A game master prepares a setting the same way a writer does in Story Mode: the "story" is the next session. Use `!` with the session as the story unit:

```
! Session 4 needs a reason for the party to enter the marshes
= The only doctor who can treat the fever lives in the marsh village
~ The marsh village refuses entry to anyone with a Guild license
>> [L:Marsh Village|closed to Guild] [N:Doctor Helve|exiled]
```

After the session, facts that emerged at the table come back into the log with `via: play` (see §5.5).

### 8.4 History and Timelines

Historical events use `[Hist:]` tags with a date category. A search for `[Hist:` then gives an unordered list, and a sort by the `year` value gives a timeline:

```
? Why are the roads north abandoned?
= The empire stopped maintaining them after a plague
~ The Guild owes its power to the collapse of overland trade
>> [Hist:Fall of the Roads|year:-212] [F:Cistern Guild|origin:road collapse]
```

### 8.5 Languages, Names, and Terms

The same loop works for naming systems and constructed languages: a question about the language, a decision, the irregularity or conflict it creates, and its ripple through the lexicon.

```
? How does the city's language form the word for a guild member?
= Suffix -ren on the craft noun: cistern-ren, ward-ren
~ Old families use the archaic -reth, and resent the new form
>> [Term:-ren|agent suffix] [Term:-reth|archaic, prestige]
```

### 8.6 Writing Your Own Conventions

If your practice needs a new prefix, tag type, or test, declare it in the world header and use it consistently. Keep the five core symbols intact. The core is what makes one person's log readable to another.

## 9. Complete Examples

The same world, logged at three levels of detail.

### 9.1 Minimal Shorthand

One cycle per line, for fast sessions:

```
C1 ?water = Guild wells ~ dry-season riots >> [F:Guild] ?enforcers
C2 ?enforcers = wardens per arrest ~ false arrests >> [Tension:False arrests] ?protection
C3 !Ch3 gate stop = passes required ~ forged passes >> [L:North Gate]
```

### 9.2 Digital Log (LWM Exploration Mode)

````markdown
---
title: The Cistern Cities
method: LWM Exploration Mode
genre: Low fantasy, urban
pitch: A drought-stricken city where water is law and the law is for sale
central_tension: The Guild keeps the city alive and keeps it poor
start_date: 2026-09-25
---

# The Cistern Cities

## Build 1
*Date: 2026-09-25 | Duration: 45m | Phase: Seed | Cycles: C1-C3*

### C1 *Seed: Section I, Power*
````

## 10. Best Practices

**Do: start every addition from a trigger.** A `=` with nothing above it is usually padding.

**Do: write at least one friction per cycle.** If you cannot find one, ask who pays, who resists, and what breaks.

**Do: keep one fact per line.** Single facts can be tagged, confirmed, or retracted one at a time.

**Do: retract, never erase.** `x=` plus `(why: ...)` is how the log stays honest.

**Do: let ripples generate questions.** The `?` lines after a ripple are the backlog of tomorrow.

**Don't: build symmetrically.** One detailed faction does not oblige you to detail the others. Wait for a trigger.

**Don't: copy the world document into the log.** The document holds the current state; the log holds the decisions. Link them with `§` references.

**Don't: resolve every tension.** A `[Tension:|resolved]` tag deserves a check: who lost, and are they still angry?

| --- | --- | --- |

## Appendix B. FAQ

**Q: Do I need a world document to use Lorelog?** A: No. The log alone can hold a world. A document is useful when the world grows and you want its current state in one place.

**Q: Can I use Lorelog and Lonelog in the same file?** A: Yes, but keep them in separate sections. The tags are shared on purpose; the core symbols are not. A `=` in Lorelog is a fact, while `=>` in Lonelog is a consequence of play.

**Q: What if an answer has no friction?** A: Some facts are neutral, such as the color of the sky. Most facts that matter have a cost for someone. If a cycle ends without `~`, look again before you move on.

**Q: Should every idea go into the log?** A: Every idea you commit to, yes. Ideas you only like go into `(parked: ...)` notes until a trigger asks for them.

**Q: How do I share a world log with readers who do not know Lorelog?** A: Share the Quick Start (§1.4). The five symbols are enough to read most logs.

## Appendix C. Symbol Design

- **`?`** is the universal sign of a question, and matches Lonelog.
- **`!`** signals demand: a story needs something, now.
- **`=`** states that something is so. It reads as "is" or "equals".
- **`~`** is a wave, the shape of instability. Friction is where the world stops being still.
- **`>>`** shows propagation: the fact flows outward into the rest of the world.

In digital files, always use code blocks: `=` under a text line and `>>` at the start of a line have Markdown meanings outside them.
